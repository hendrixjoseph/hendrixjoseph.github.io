I'm serving as Treasurer for [my county's Libertarian political party](https://montgomery.lpo.org/). Recently the county board of elections emailed me asking for some documentation about a few old expenses.

I don't have any problem with providing the documentation. I'm not the kind of libertarian that is more contrarian than anything else. Heck, I value transparency. (Although what extra transparency is provided by providing proof that we spent money versus us just reporting that we spent money?)

But I wondered - did I have to provide the documentation? Was it a legal requirement? Was it supposed to be originally provided?

So I asked ChatGPT. But not just "am I supposed to?" The thing is, I don't trust any LLM response, especially to such a legal question. So I not only asked it *am I legally required* but also asked it to provide sources.

And then I checked those sources. I don't want it hallucinating sources.

In the end, yes, I am supposed to provide documentation. "Receipts." Which can be anything from a cancelled check, a bank statement with the necessary info, or a receipted bill (whatever than means).

In case you're curious, here's my prompt:

> I'm in Ohio filing finance reports for my local political party as the treasurer. The county election commission is asking for receipts for various expenses. Is providing these receipts a legal requirement, and if so, where is that specified?

And here's ChatGPT's response:

> Yes. **For an Ohio political party, there is a statutory requirement to provide supporting documentation for expenditures over $25.** The relevant provisions are in Ohio Revised Code Chapter 3517 and the Ohio Administrative Code.
> 
> ### The key statute: R.C. 3517.10
> 
> Ohio Revised Code § 3517.10 applies specifically to **political parties**, among other political entities. It says:
> 
> > "Every expenditure in excess of twenty-five dollars shall be vouched for by a receipted bill, stating the purpose of the expenditure, that shall be filed with the statement of expenditures."
> 
> It also says that **a canceled check with a notation of the purpose of the expenditure qualifies as a receipted bill**. ([Ohio Laws][1])
> 
> So, if your county political party reports, for example:
> 
> * $50 printing expense
> * $125 catering expense
> * $300 advertising expense
> 
> those expenditures need supporting documentation.
> 
> [Ohio Revised Code § 3517.10](https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-3517.10)
> 
> ### There's an important distinction about what counts as a "receipt"
> 
> The Ohio Administrative Code provides more detail in **OAC 111:2-4-13**.
> 
> It says that expenditures must be recorded with the:
> 
> * name and address of the payee
> * date
> * amount
> * purpose of the expenditure
> 
> And it specifically says that records related to expenditure activity must be retained.
> 
> For an expenditure over $25, the rule says the requirement can be satisfied with **any one of**:
> 
> 1. A receipted bill
> 2. A canceled check
> 3. A bank account statement containing the payee, amount, date, and check number
> 
> That's potentially significant in your situation. **The law doesn't necessarily require an itemized store receipt for every expenditure.** A canceled check or qualifying bank statement can satisfy the statutory "vouching" requirement.
> 
> [OAC 111:2-4-13 — Accounting for contributions and expenditures](https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-administrative-code/rule-111%3A2-4-13)

From what I understand, ChatGPT was tweaked a little while back to provide sources, so it may have done so even had I not asked. Regardless, I still found it important to actually *check* the sources.

I've deep-linked the following two links to the Ohio Revised Code and Ohio Administrative Code, but in case that deep-link doesn't work, the link text is also what is deep linked:

* Section 3517.10 (D) (4) [Every expenditure in excess of twenty-five dollars shall be vouched for by a receipted bill](https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-3517.10#:~:text=Every%20expenditure%20in%20excess%20of%20twenty%2Dfive%20dollars%20shall%20be%20vouched%20for%20by%20a%20receipted%20bill)
* Rule 111:2-4-13 (D) [The requirement of section 3517.10 of the Revised Code that every expenditure in excess of twenty-five dollars shall be vouched for, is satisfied by providing an original or photocopy of any of the following](https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-administrative-code/rule-111%3A2-4-13#:~:text=The%20requirement%20of%20section%203517.10%20of%20the%20Revised%20Code%20that%20every%20expenditure%20in%20excess%20of%20twenty%2Dfive%20dollars%20shall%20be%20vouched%20for%2C%20is%20satisfied%20by%20providing%20an%20original%20or%20photocopy%20of%20any%20of%20the%20following)

And, in case you're wondering, I did provide the documentation to the Board of Elections, and I will keep in mind that $25 requirement for the future.
